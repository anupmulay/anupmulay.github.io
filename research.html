Work in progress

The Welfare Costs of Distortions with Imperfectly Contestable Markets

with Anthony Savagar

Studies how distortions such as markups and returns to scale affect welfare when firm entry is slow to erode quasi rents. Presents evidence that entry speed differs across US industries: hospitality adjusts quickly, construction slowly. A dynamic entry model suggests sluggish entry increases the welfare costs of these distortions.

An E-DSGE for the Baltics

with Patrick Grüning

An energy DSGE model covering fossil fuels alongside green and brown energy production. The model is calibrated separately for Latvia and Lithuania, and assesses the transition effects of climate specific fiscal and macroprudential policies.

Capital Portfolio, Collateral Constraints and the Measured Efficiency Wedge

with Alfred Duncan

A general equilibrium model with differentiated, substitutable capital assets, where financing depends on balance sheet composition. After a negative financial shock, firms shift from expensive long term assets to easily financed short term assets. This reallocation appears to raise measured TFP, with supporting evidence in US data.

LITMAS

LIThuanian Model for Analysis of Scenarios

LITMAS is an estimated DSGE model of the Lithuanian economy and the Bank's primary tool for scenario analysis. Stefan Girstmair and I develop and estimate it at Lietuvos bankas. Together with NMCM, the Bank's semistructural model, it feeds into the Bank's regular forecasting rounds. I also use it in the Bank's policy scenario work, which I lead. Recent questions range from oil price paths to government budget analysis.

Ongoing

E-LITMAS

Energy LITMAS

An extension of LITMAS with an energy sector that models fossil fuels alongside green and brown energy production. I developed E-LITMAS and continue to calibrate and maintain it. The Bank uses it for scenario analysis of energy and climate policy, most recently for energy price shocks arising from the crisis in the Middle East.

Productivity drivers in Lithuania

with Darius Imbrasas, Jose Garcia-Louzao, Kasparas Vasiliauskas, Matas Gaigalas, Kipras Kalesninkas, and Rokas Budrauskas · Lietuvos bankas Occasional Paper No. 64/2026

An assessment of what has driven labour productivity growth in Lithuania and why it has slowed in recent years. The paper combines macroeconomic, sectoral and firm level evidence. I wrote the section on sector level labour reallocation. It uses CompNet data to split productivity growth in each sector into gains within firms and the reallocation of labour between firms.

An Ex-Ante Assessment of the Proposal to Reform the Second Pillar of the Lithuanian Pension System

with Karolis Bielskis, Federica Brenna, Jose Garcia-Louzao, and Stefan Girstmair · Lietuvos bankas Occasional Paper No. 57/2025

An assessment of the proposal to allow withdrawals from the second pillar of the Lithuanian pension system, made ahead of the policy taking effect. We quantify the medium term macroeconomic impact of alternative withdrawal scenarios using LITMAS, and assess the long run consequences for pension replacement rates.

The Demography of Currency Unions

with Swapnil Singh

Members of a currency union age at different rates. This project asks how that affects the transmission of a common monetary policy across members. We build a two country HANK OLG model of a currency union, combining an exact demographic transition with a structural general equilibrium block.
