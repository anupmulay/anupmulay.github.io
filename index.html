About Me

I hold a PhD in Economics from the University of Kent, and my work lies at the intersection of macroeconomic theory, computation and policy. My research deals with questions such as the welfare costs of market power when firm entry is slow (with Anthony Savagar), how collateral constraints shape firms' capital portfolios (with Alfred Duncan), and how demographic change affects the transmission of monetary policy in a currency union (with Swapnil Singh).

As a Principal Economist in the Applied Macroeconomic Research Division at Lietuvos bankas, I bring these tools to policy. I built LITMAS, the Bank's large scale estimated DSGE model (with Stefan Girstmair). I then independently extended it into E-LITMAS, the Bank's model for energy and climate policy analysis. I maintain and develop NMCM, the Bank's primary semistructural forecasting model. I lead the Bank's analysis of several policy questions, most recently the macroeconomic effects of energy price shocks arising from the crisis in the Middle East, which I modelled with E-LITMAS. I have contributed to others, such as the quantification of the proposed reform to the second pillar of the Lithuanian pension system.

I also lecture Economic Theory II, an intermediate macroeconomics course, at Vilnius University.

Research interests Structural and semistructural modelling, macroeconometrics, microeconometrics (panel data), computational methods, heterogeneous agent models.
