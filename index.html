About Me

I am a Principal Economist in the Applied Macroeconomic Research Division at Lietuvos bankas, where I specialise in structural and semistructural macroeconomic modelling and its application to policy analysis.

I am responsible for developing several of the Bank's core macroeconomic models. Stefan Girstmair and I built LITMAS, the LIThuanian Model for Analysis of Scenarios, a large scale estimated DSGE model that we continue to develop and estimate. I independently extended it into E-LITMAS, the version of the model the Bank uses for energy and climate policy, which adds a full energy sector covering fossil fuels alongside green and brown energy production. I also maintain and develop NMCM, the Bank's primary semistructural forecasting model. Beyond the Bank's core models, I am building a two country HANK OLG model of a currency union with Swapnil Singh to study how demographic change shapes the transmission of monetary policy.

These models come together in the Bank's policy work, where I lead its scenario analysis. Using LITMAS and NMCM, I address the questions most pressing for policymakers, from the macroeconomic effects of oil price swings to fiscal measures and alternative government budget paths. Recent applications include a quantification of the proposed reform to the second pillar of the Lithuanian pension system, in which my colleagues and I simulated opt out scenarios and their effects on GDP and inflation through 2028 (Lietuvos bankas Occasional Paper No. 57/2025). With E-LITMAS, I have modelled energy price shock scenarios arising from the crisis in the Middle East, alongside broader climate and energy transition scenarios for the Baltic economies. LITMAS and NMCM also feed into the Bank's regular forecasting rounds, providing the baseline projections against which these scenarios are assessed.

My academic foundation lies in macroeconomic theory and computation. I hold a PhD in Economics from the University of Kent, where my thesis examined how financial frictions affect measured aggregate productivity, and I continue to pursue this line of research alongside my policy work. On the computational side, I explore and compare solution methods for structural models, and on the empirical side, I decompose total factor productivity to trace how frictions in credit and capital markets distort the signals on which policymakers rely.

My career has spanned research, policy and teaching. Before joining Lietuvos bankas, I held postdoctoral and research positions at the University of Kent, where my postdoctoral work involved extensive microeconometric and panel data analysis of UK business microdata in Stata and R. At the National Institute of Economic and Social Research (NIESR), I transferred and rewrote the code of a nowcasting model for a current version of MATLAB. I now lecture Economic Theory II, an intermediate macroeconomics course, at Vilnius University, having previously taught financial economics, money and banking, and data analysis as a teaching assistant at the University of Kent.

My current research addresses three questions. With Anthony Savagar, I study the welfare costs of market power when slow firm entry allows incumbents to retain quasi rents. With Alfred Duncan, I examine how collateral constraints shape firms' capital portfolios and, in turn, measured efficiency. With Patrick Grüning, I analyse the transition effects of climate specific fiscal and macroprudential policies in the Baltic economies.

Research interests Structural and semistructural modelling, macroeconometrics, microeconometrics (panel data), computational methods, heterogeneous agent models.
